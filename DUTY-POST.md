## LinkedIn

```
"Why is the same car twice the price in India?"

I got tired of guessing, so I built the answer into a side project.

A ₹55 lakh imported SUV breaks down as:

Product value (CIF, landed)      ₹20,71,563
Basic customs duty @ 70%         ₹14,50,094
Social welfare surcharge @ 10%    ₹1,45,009
Compensation cess @ 22%           ₹8,06,667
IGST @ 28%                       ₹10,26,667
                                 ───────────
You pay                          ₹55,00,000

62% of the price is duty and tax.

Two things I learned building it:

1. MRP in India is inclusive of all taxes by law. So tax is never added at
   checkout — it's extracted from the listed price. I initially built it the
   wrong way round, which would have looked fake to any Indian shopper.

2. The social welfare surcharge is 10% *of the duty*, not of the value. On a
   70% duty that compounds, and it's the line nobody accounts for.

Category rates vary more than you'd expect: toys carry 70% duty (raised
deliberately to push local manufacturing), private aircraft only 2.5%, and
printed books are both duty-free and GST-exempt — a book's breakdown shows
zero government take.

Built into thodasa.com — a reels-style shopping concept. Every product shows
where its price actually goes.
```

## Notes before posting

- **The number is defensible but say "indicative".** Real landed cost also
  carries freight, insurance, importer and dealer margin — the breakdown
  attributes the non-tax remainder to "product value", which bundles those in.
  If someone in the replies is an importer, concede that quickly and precisely;
  it's the one fair challenge.
- Slabs are real but simplified: car BCD varies by engine size, CIF value and
  CBU/CKD status. "Indicative slabs" is already on the card.
- **Post the screenshot as native media**, not a link — X suppresses link-first
  posts. Put thodasa.com in the first reply if reach matters more than clicks.
- Best window: 9–11 PM IST.
